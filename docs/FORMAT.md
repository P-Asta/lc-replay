# LCReplay schema 1

## Capture work scheduling (0.25.24)

Schema 1 and LCIX0007 remain unchanged. A live snapshot keeps entity, held-item and view poses at one observation time; its immutable frame and state-transition records are published on later work frames. State transitions retain the snapshot timestamp. Sparse animation and visual events use their own observation timestamps, and event/world arrival order may still differ from timestamp order.

Storage/event catch-up and maintenance have cooperative elapsed-time and item-count budgets. Only one live-snapshot, snapshot-output or maintenance stage is selected per tick, with a small independent animation scan. The game frame does not replay a backlog of missed samples. Unity operations such as one mesh/texture read or the synchronized pose pass remain indivisible; these are not hard real-time guarantees. Completion streams already captured trailing records asynchronously before writing the end marker.

These capture budgets apply only while recording. Playback keeps its separate scene-building budget, asynchronous window decoding and per-render actor updates. While a large world record is in flight, capture admits a bounded motion tail with the same 48-record/4 MiB pause and 12-record/1 MiB resume watermarks; the world payload alone does not freeze motion sampling.

Material texture references are registered before export. A world is published after one-at-a-time async GPU readback and worker PNG encoding resolves those references. RGBA bytes are copied in the GPU completion callback because request memory lasts one frame; pooled managed bytes belong exclusively to the encoder until it returns them. Only owned byte arrays reach the worker. Cancellation releases submitted surfaces through callbacks and does not wait for the GPU. Stopping after geometry collection preserves completed textures and removes unfinished texture references, retaining material colors. Devices without async readback keep a synchronous readback fallback with worker PNG encoding.

## Lighter capture queue (0.25.23)

Schema 1 and LCIX0007 remain unchanged. Production output uses a 64-record/16 MiB estimated payload queue and an 8 MiB ordered overflow. Capture pauses early at 48 records or 4 MiB and resumes at 12 records/1 MiB with no pending overflow. One oversized record is allowed in an empty writer. Pending admission is limited to 16 records and a cooperative 0.75 ms budget per tick; hook catch-up is limited to 48 events. Accepted records finish asynchronously, while pressure may reduce new sampling density. These limits do not cap the full process heap.

## Capture storage pressure (0.25.22)

Schema 1 and LCIX0007 remain unchanged. JSON is serialized through a bounded UTF-8 stream directly into gzip. A bounded ordered overflow retains records refused by the disk queue. Capture pauses before gathering further snapshots while that backlog drains; completion awaits accepted records asynchronously.

capture/storage-gap events carry seconds and droppedEvents. They mark periods with reduced sampling and bounded method-event overflow; sound waveforms are still not recorded. Optional sidecar I/O errors allow the primary file to continue and the index to rebuild at playback. A persistent primary output failure leaves its complete physical prefix, with an incomplete archive status and recovered duration/bookmark metadata.
## Native player IK and quota day calculation (0.25.21)

Schema 1 and LCIX0007 are unchanged. Player animation still consists of sparse controller state/parameter events. Playback selects the verified local/remote player controller recorded in those events and puts its controller output before the RigBuilder PreviousInputs outputs in the same manual graph. Native IK therefore consumes the sampled pose without depending on a separate engine animation update. No new bone or sound payload is introduced.

New quota metadata calculates deadlineDaysRemaining with floor(timeUntilDeadline / totalTime), matching TimeOfDay.UpdateProfitQuotaCurrentTime. Its displayed day remains clamped to 1..3. Previous metadata and file names are preserved; this correction does not relabel already written recordings.
## Shared native player references (0.25.20)

`GeometrySnapshot.PrefabKey=player` and `PrefabRendererPath` identify a matching renderer under the installed StartOfRound player prefab. Capture verifies exact shared mesh identity and renderer path; native vertices, bind poses and default maps are supplied at playback. Changed materials retain their snapshots, and unmatched custom player meshes retain ordinary geometry capture. Schema 1 and the index format are unchanged. Player pose, action and per-player state remain independent.

## Actual static emitter actions (0.25.19)

The `native-ambient-sound-events` header capability disables template-derived structure audio. Static emitters use existing `sound/play` and `sound/stop` events with an empty `EntityId`, `ambient=true` and a transient `source` key prefixed by `ambient:`. Play stores the installed `clip` name, loop flag, volume/pitch, native min/max distance and rolloff, loop offset and emitter `x/y/z`. Points are world-space unless `anchor=ship-elevator`, in which case they are relative to that recorded moving root. Custom attenuation uses 16 finite scalar samples. No audio waveform or continuous emitter position stream is added.

Only observed playback and changed state authorize a loop. PlayOneShot does not authorize the source's configured looping clip. Static sound keys retain their last state across indexed windows independently of actor retirement. Schema 1 and `LCIX0007` remain compatible. Old recordings without emitter actions omit unverified rain, entrance and campfire ambience; only the explicitly identified native breaker-box hum fallback remains. Earlier weather/template policies below describe their historical releases and are superseded by this source-based policy.

## Weather, apparatus and circuit bees (0.25.18)

Schema 1 remains compatible. Round state adds `currentLevelWeather`; item state can include `isLungDocked`. Circuit-bee state can include the `hive` entity reference, native `beesZappingMode`, `$beeZapSeed` and `$beeTarget` (`hive`, `self` or `override`). Only the rare override stores `$beeTargetPosition`; ordinary hive targets use the reconstructed item pose. No bee particle positions, extra bone stream or audio waveform is introduced.

Older bee captures without a hive link choose a nearby active hive for idle swarms. Their exact original line-of-sight decision is unavailable. Unknown weather suppresses unproven rain ambience instead of starting every default-active installed template source. Historical apparatus loops use held/ship state when docking state is absent.

## State-gated effects (0.25.17)

Schema 1 remains compatible. Item state can include `attaching` and `finishedAttaching` for HauntedMaskItem, and hazard state can include `mineActivated`. These are scalar state flags, not new animation or waveform streams. Older mask recordings use their native `attaching` Animator parameter when present; ordinary masks default to unlit eyes.

Turret sound loops are validated against the sampled hazard state before restoration and while playing. Passive ambience uses only eligible active installed sources; controller-owned effects require action/state playback. Landmine idle visuals use the installed Animator with animation events disabled and replay-clock advancement.

## Native replay effects (0.25.16)

Schema 1 remains compatible. Item scalar state may include `itemProperties.positionOffset`, `itemProperties.rotationOffset` and `itemProperties.twoHandedAnimation`, allowing a modded item to use its original hold offsets. Player state adds essential vehicle/interaction/inspection flags used by the native IK constraints; hazard state may include `enteringBerserkMode`. No additional bone stream or audio waveform is introduced. Hazard sound events may use `hazard:Turret` / `hazard:Landmine` prefab identities, including controller-referenced sibling sources.

Particle poses interpolate by emitter, seed and birth; expired particles disappear and future particles are not emitted early. Native turret child effects, structure loop ambience and FlyingBugs graphs are reconstructed from installed assets. Historical waveform recordings suppress derived audio to prevent doubling. Camera-local night-vision lights are reconstructed once from the installed game, rather than serialized as ordinary world fixtures.

## Bookmarks, native actors and daylight (0.25.14)

`recording-bookmarks` identifies `marker/bookmark` events, whose timestamps enter the compact index without carrying into future windows. Small archive segment manifests contain `BookmarkCount`; day totals sum their segments. `game-clock` adds optional `gameStartNormalizedTime`, `gameStartTime` and `gameNumberOfHours` header metadata and round scalar fields. The native clock uses the game's six-AM offset and number of hours.

`sparse-sun-lighting` marks `lighting/sun` events, keyed independently by `sunDirect` / `sunIndirect`. The event stores installed light ID/name and eleven invariant-culture scalar values: intensity, light/shadow dimmer, RGBA and XYZW rotation. Capture samples the two lights at most once per second and emits only changes; replay interpolates neighboring states. The live player's indoor indirect-light dimmer is excluded. `LightSnapshot.Name` is optional and helps match native scene sunlight. No lightmap or audio payload is added. Older installed-scene files can evaluate the original isolated sun Animator using the already-recorded normalized game time. No shared/live TimeOfDay is advanced.

Players and enemies use full inert native animation hierarchies and IK targets. Native skin renderer coordinates replace flattened snapshot renderer offsets. New default player captures retain states/parameters rather than learned bone tracks. Explicit full-bone capture and historical track records remain readable.

## Metadata indexing (0.25.13)

Playback and duration queries use metadata scanning when a usable `.lci` is absent. `IndexSingleFile(..., deferPayloadValidation: true)` inflates frame envelopes only through kind/time and world envelopes through capture-set ID, then seeks to the next compressed record. Headers and event descriptors are decoded normally. The strict scan remains the default for inspection callers. Physical size, record counts, expanded-size budgets, time ordering and metadata limits still apply. Every selected `ReadWindow` payload is fully decoded, validated and checked against its index before use. A corrupt future payload therefore fails when that window is opened, without delaying a valid first section. This does not change the `.lcr` schema or `LCIX0006` sidecar format.

Schema 1 is retained. `native-sound-events` marks action-based audio: `sound/play` stores installed `clip` name, transient `source` ID, owner entity, loop flag, volume, pitch, spatial blend, distance/rolloff and optional loop start `offset`; `sound/stop` stops that source. Version 0.25.12 adds optional owning `prefab`, serialized clip-field/array `asset`, source transform `path`, AudioSource `component` index and owner-local source position `x/y/z`. These identify the installed effect and original source curve without storing audio buffers. Older events remain readable and enemy clip lookup is restricted to its own prefab. No sample buffers, PCM/ADPCM or voice blocks are produced. Loops carry their last play/stop state across windows; old one-shots do not replay on seek. Historical waveform records documented below remain readable.

`GeometrySnapshot.PrefabKey` and `PrefabRendererPath` reference native item/enemy assets. Their mesh arrays may be empty; matching installed assets supply vertices, skin bindings, rest scales and Animator controller/avatar. Runtime custom material snapshots take priority. Optional `Uvs1`, `Uvs2`, `Uvs3` and `Tangents` preserve secondary shader channels, with normal/tangent vectors transformed when compacting static batches. Optional `TextureSnapshot.Name` helps select the correct installed map before PNG fallback.

The `world-postfx-without-player-filters` capability identifies captures made without HUD personal-filter volumes. World fog/exposure/grading remains; drunkness, fear, poison, flashbang, underwater, death filters, distortion, chromatic aberration, motion blur, depth of field and vignette are excluded. Older merged post-processing changes cannot be separated reliably, so playback retains their initial grading and ignores subsequent personal-filter grading updates.

The current sidecar magic is `LCIX0007`. Stateful descriptors use codes 1–5 for visual, animation, item pose, post-processing and native sound loops, 6 for death, 7 for bookmark and 8 for sunlight. Bookmark/death descriptors have empty keys; sunlight has an independent state key. UTF-8 keys have a little-endian UInt16 byte length. Later windows load the latest independent states and nearby boundary states for animation blending. Bookmark times are available without decoding history. Playback trims historical animation/item/sound state for absent actors. Older indexes rebuild once. Window budgets remain 4 MiB initially and 48 MiB afterward, with bounded world payloads. Shared indexed world payloads stay immutable.


Version 0.25.1 retains schema 1. `TextureSnapshot.Linear` defaults to false and preserves linear data-map encoding in new captures. A room may have up to eight `AdditionalVolumes`, each with finite positive `Size` and finite `Center`; these are separate containment volumes, including the mine's special entrance region, not a combined enclosing box. Frames optionally include bounded `ParticleStyles` keyed by emitter ID; particle poses may refer to these IDs and add world velocity, 3D size/rotation, lifetime, remaining lifetime and random seed. Styles identify installed source effects and renderer state. Turret bullets use sampled particles while firing, rather than a loop that can outlive the source effect. Lines add optional material/shader identity, texture mode and alignment. Missing fields preserve the older read behavior, but cannot recover source effect identities absent from old recordings.

Playback decodes independent records using a bounded worker batch and yields them in file order. `ReplayFileIndex.ReuseWorldPayloads` is opt-in: returned world payloads and their children must be treated as read-only. The cache holds only weak references, checks the completed file's length/write timestamp, creates independent per-window record timestamps and revalidates reused payloads.

Each `.lcr` file starts with the eight ASCII bytes `LCREPL01`. Records follow without padding:

| Field | Encoding |
| --- | --- |
| Compressed payload length | Positive little-endian signed 32-bit integer |
| Expanded payload length | Positive little-endian signed 32-bit integer |
| Payload | One independent gzip member containing UTF-8 JSON |

The first payload is a `ReplayRecord` with `Kind: "header"`, `Time: 0`, and `Header.SchemaVersion: 1`. Other records have exactly one payload matching `Kind`: `frame`, `event`, or `world`. The writer closes a successful recording with `Kind: "end"`, which has no nested payload. JSON member names match the C# DTO properties. Quaternion order is X, Y, Z, W. Mesh vertex arrays contain consecutive X, Y, Z components; triangle arrays index vertices, not components. World and entity transforms use the recorder's coordinate conventions; bone poses are local to their parent. `EntitySnapshot.Renderers` contains optional `RenderPose` entries whose position, rotation and scale are relative to the entity root. Each renderer ID matches the corresponding `GeometrySnapshot.Id`; its `Active` flag captures visibility. Older schema-1 files without renderer poses load with an empty renderer list.

Version 0.20 retains schema 1. Audio `source-block` events add `global`: only genuinely global music/UI remains two-dimensional in playback; world sources use the spectator camera's position and the recorded distance settings. Sparse `visual` events store `renderer` visibility changes and `spray` HDRP decal projector poses/colors, keyed by capture-set ID. The earlier `.lci` sidecar used `LCIX0002` and marked visual records so bounded playback windows could carry prior visual state without reading older audio. New recordings keep tombstones for destroyed moving renderers, and emission properties are prioritized in the existing bounded material snapshots.

Version 0.14 adds optional `ReplayFrame.SceneRenderers`: bounded world-space poses for generated furniture and doors without an entity owner. `GeometrySnapshot.IsMovingSceneRenderer` identifies these surfaces, and `Active` stores initial visibility. `GeometrySnapshot.RootBonePath` preserves the original skin root for animated enemy meshes. New audio `source-block` events include invariant-culture `x`, `y`, `z`, `spatial`, `min`, `max`, and `rolloff` fields; old events without these fields retain 2D mixed playback. All additions retain schema 1 and default to legacy behavior when absent.

New headers may advertise `sparse-entity-renderer-poses`. In those files, `EntitySnapshot.Renderers` contains only child renderers that differ from the matching `GeometrySnapshot` transform or initial visibility. A renderer absent from the list uses its world snapshot baseline; a destroyed child is carried as an explicit pose with `Active: false`. Older headers retain complete-list semantics, where a missing child ID in a complete list means it was destroyed.

Version 0.15 adds optional `ReplayFrame.Lines`: at most 128 world-space LineRenderer paths per frame, each with 2–32 finite points, end colors, widths and an indoor flag. Matching paths interpolate between frames; missing paths appear or disappear at sample boundaries. Older schema-1 recordings read this as an empty list. New recordings also tag entity-owned geometry with its dungeon room when it is spatially inside a generated tile.

Version 0.16 keeps schema 1. New `audio/source-block` events optionally include `player=true|false` to mark sources attached to a player or held item, and `capture=clip|tap` to identify readable-clip fallback for a virtualized source versus an audio callback. The header advertises `player-audio-origin` and `virtual-audio-readable-clips`. Older events lack these keys and continue playing; their player-origin sounds cannot be muted reliably. Each block still uses the bounded mono ADPCM format and source position/distance fields described below.

Version 0.3 adds optional embedded appearance fields without changing schema 1. `WorldSnapshot.Textures` contains unique IDs, dimensions and PNG bytes (base64 in JSON); `Materials` contains unique IDs, color, a texture ID, UV scale/offset and alpha cutoff. Geometry may contain UV pairs, normal triples, submesh index arrays, material IDs and skin data. Skin data contains entity-relative bone paths, 16 column-major matrix values per bind pose, and four bone indices/weights per vertex. Missing appearance arrays default to empty, preserving older files. No game asset paths or executable Unity objects are serialized.

Version 0.4 adds optional `GeometrySnapshot.MeshSourceId`, `IsInterior` and `RoomId` fields. A nonempty `MeshSourceId` refers to an **earlier static, non-proxy geometry entry in the same world**. An instance retains its own transform, visibility and material references but must have empty vertex, triangle, submesh, UV, normal and skin arrays. Missing, forward, self, cyclic, chained-instance and skinned-source references are rejected. This permits repeated room geometry to share one stored mesh without copying its vertex data into every instance. Empty defaults preserve older schema-1 files.

Version 0.5 adds optional `ReplayFrame.Anchors`, `GeometrySnapshot.AnchorId` and `WorldSnapshot.Lights` without changing schema 1. Anchor poses give moving environment roots a sampled world-space transform; a geometry/light anchor ID stores its pose relative to that root. The recorder currently uses the game's ship/elevator and ship animator roots. Light snapshots contain bounded type, transform, color, intensity, range and shadow settings. Older files load with empty anchor/light lists; playback uses the earlier unlit appearance for a world without recorded lights.

Version 0.25.1 adds optional `LightSnapshot.BakeType`, `ShadowStrength`, `LightDimmer` and `ShadowDimmer`. New recordings distinguish realtime shadows from baked and mixed fixtures and retain the source light/shadow attenuation. Older files have no bake mode; playback disables their unverified realtime shadows to avoid inventing occlusion.

Captured custom passes optionally include `MaterialName` and `FetchColorBuffer` so replay can identify their installed material and color-buffer setting. Missing values retain legacy decoding. Playback currently suppresses `FullScreen/SpongePosterizeNew` because its detached-camera buffer sampling produces black surface artifacts.

Version 0.6 adds optional `WorldSnapshot.CaptureSetId`, `Layer`, `Rooms` and `Environment`, plus material render queues, shader keywords and bounded property values. A capture set has separate exterior chunks and an interior record; playback merges records with the same capture-set ID before replacing the displayed world. `Rooms` store generated tile bounds used to decide whether the spectator is indoors. `Environment` stores blended HDRP sky, fog, exposure and color-grading parameters, with six bounded PNG cubemap faces when available. New captures set `SkyFaceEncoding=rgbe8` and store linear HDR color as RGBE bytes in those PNGs; older faces have no encoding marker and use their original 8-bit color interpretation. An all-black old cubemap uses the recorded ambient sky color as a gradient fallback. Playback creates a private HDRP volume and does not serialize Unity components or shaders. Empty capture-set IDs and missing environment/room data preserve older recordings.

Version 0.8 adds optional `GeometrySnapshot.Instances`, natural LOD metadata (`LodGroupId`, `LodLevel`, `LodCenter`, `LodSwitchDistance`) and `WorldSnapshot.ParticleEmitters` without changing schema 1. `Instances` is a flat array of at most 8,192 affine, finite, world-space 4×4 matrices for GPU-instanced meshes such as grass. Natural LOD entries carry a group ID and a near/far level selected by the spectator camera; unpaired entries fall back to their available level. Particle emitters store bounded looping-effect settings, not per-particle or GPU VFX state. Missing fields default to empty, preserving older recordings.

Version 0.9 adds optional `WorldSnapshot.LocalFogs` without changing schema 1. Each entry stores a bounded, active HDRP local volumetric fog volume's transform, color, density, size, face fades and distance fade. Texture and material masks are not embedded. Missing entries default to an empty list, so older recordings load but cannot recover local fog that was never captured.

Version 0.10 extends each optional local fog entry with mask mode, tiling, scrolling speed and a bounded raw RGBA 3D density mask when its source texture is CPU-readable. Each axis is limited to 64 voxels and the total mask budget is 4 MiB per world; missing masks default to empty. The reader validates exact RGBA byte count. Ground textures and distance-detail policies are playback decisions and do not change schema 1.

Version 0.11 keeps schema 1 but writes one physical `.lcr` per quota/deadline day. A new header marks `singleFile=true`. The file continues to contain independently compressed records until an `end` marker; no intermediate header or numbered file is inserted. A companion `.lci` stores record offsets and timestamps for fast opening; it can be rebuilt from the `.lcr`. Playback reads bounded virtual windows on demand with the preceding frame and latest world capture set. Older multipart files keep their existing reader and timeline. Ground color layers may now be captured at up to 2048 pixels per edge, with a 32 MiB PNG budget per world and 96 MiB expanded/48 MiB compressed per-record bounds. Unity Terrain without a MeshRenderer uses up to a 128×128 cell height field, sampled normals and UVs, plus an available terrain-layer texture.

Version 0.12 adds optional `WorldSnapshot.AssetScene`, `AssetGameVersion`, `AssetBuildIndex`, `AssetRendererPaths`, `AssetTerrainPaths`, `MapSeed`, `LevelId`, `DungeonSeed` and `DungeonFlow` fields without changing schema 1. A bounded component path is a sequence of transform sibling indices followed by component type and index. Paths refer only to renderers and Terrain components that were already present when the built-in moon scene loaded; procedural additions and moving objects retain embedded snapshots. Playback resolves those paths against an inert additive copy of the *same installed game version's* scene. Missing or mismatched moon assets cannot be recovered from the compact record and produce a playback error. Map/dungeon settings are recorded as provenance, not used to regenerate the dungeon. An environment-only exterior record may share a capture-set ID with earlier geometry records to update sky/fog after landing without duplicating the map.

Current recordings also keep the blended scalar HDRP settings used by the gameplay camera. `Environment.CustomPasses` stores up to four enabled fullscreen passes (shader name, injection point, material pass and up to 32 scalar/color/vector properties); `CustomPassCaptureComplete` distinguishes a newly recorded absence from legacy files. The installed shader is required at playback. Changed filters are `postfx/component` events with a bounded `PostProcess` component payload, sampled at most four times per second and written only when effective values change. The snapshot at a seek time is the latest event for each component, or the world baseline when no earlier event exists. HDRP color curves retain at most 32 weighted keyframes per parameter; custom-pass textures are not embedded. Older files have neither the pass list nor these events; the installed v81 outline shader can supply a legacy visual fallback when present.

New recordings may include `animation/state` events for active player and enemy Animator layers. Their data contains `layer`, state `hash`, `normalizedTime`, `duration`, effective `speed`, `weight` and, when available, the dominant weighted `clip` name. Each state also carries `animatorPath` and the installed controller/avatar names, so independently animated rigs on one enemy remain separate. Sparse `animation/parameters` events contain only changed float (`f`), integer (`i`) and boolean (`b`) values keyed by the Animator parameter's numeric hash, plus `animatorPath`. Values are applied to a matching installed controller before each sampled state is evaluated, preserving game blend trees such as walk and sprint. Skinned geometry stores `RigBones` once, plus optional `AnimatorPath`, `AnimatorController` and `AnimatorAvatar` local asset keys. Players apply reusable observed tracks with native held-item constraints; enemies prefer the matching installed Animator even when an older file contains learned tracks. If neither is available, it retains the stored rig pose; `CaptureBones` can still record independent per-frame poses. Built-in entity state dictionaries contain selected gameplay fields, while explicitly configured mod types retain bounded general field capture. Older schema-1 recordings without animator paths use their first Animator. The 0.25.1 `.lci` sidecar used `LCIX0005`, with distinct visual, animation, item-pose and keyed post-processing event tags. It carries visual state only from the current world capture set, the latest value for each post-processing component and the animation and item-pose event age across windows. Older sidecars are rebuilt from their `.lcr` files on first use.

An `animation/track` event optionally contains an `AnimationTrack` payload for one observed Animator layer, state and dominant clip. It stores `Layer`, `StateHash`, `AnimatorPath`, at most 128 bone paths and 64 sorted normalized phases, with sample-major local position triples and quaternion quadruples. Layer 0 is the legacy default. The recorder learns a short motion track once per actor/Animator/layer/state/clip; later frames retain only state and changed-parameter events. Upper-layer tracks contain the actual blended game pose for actions such as spawning and emotes. Base tracks are learned only without an active clip on an upper layer, preventing an action pose from contaminating a reusable idle or walk. Interrupted base loops are discarded and can be learned from a later complete cycle. A partial upper-layer loop may be retained as a nonlooping track, holding its last observed pose rather than repeating an incomplete cycle. Scale and the rest of the rig come from `RigBones`. Track events can be written after their observation starts while retaining that start timestamp, so indexed windows carry the reusable track into relevant earlier and later windows. Older recordings without upper-layer tracks cannot recover those poses unless the installed Animator can bind their rebuilt rig.

New item frames may set `PoseFromItemEvents: true` and omit `Position`, `Rotation` and `Scale`. The latest `item/pose` event supplies either one resting pose (`Mode: rest`) or a fall (`Mode: fall`) with starting pose, target, initial `FallTime`, `FallRate` and 17 sampled points from the installed game's fall curve. `AnchorId` is `ship-elevator` when the item moves with that anchor. Held, pocketed and otherwise unpredictable items keep ordinary frame transforms. `item/drop` identifies a game-observed player drop and records the dropper when the tracked player is known. `item/impact` stores the dropped item's installed `itemId`, sound clip name and transient AudioSource instance ID; playback uses the game's drop clip when no matching recorded audio block exists. Item-pose events are carried into later indexed windows; impact events are not carried, so an old impact cannot replay on a seek.

Version 0.12.1 filters scene component paths before capture. Paths must be unique within their renderer or terrain kind and at most 256 characters; a world can reference at most 4,096 components. A surface that cannot be referenced uses the embedded-geometry capture path instead of invalidating the recording. The reader continues to reject malformed stored references.

Version 0.13 adds optional `LightSnapshot.UseColorTemperature` and `ColorTemperature` values (1,000–20,000 K), plus up to 256 `ReplayFrame.Particles` poses per frame. Particle poses contain finite world position, rotation, size, RGBA color and an indoor flag. Observed audio uses ordinary `event` records with `Category: "audio"`, `Name: "source-block"` and `rate`, `samples`, base64 `adpcm`, and `source` data. Each independent IMA-ADPCM block decodes to at most 8,192 mono samples at 22,050 Hz; the two-byte initial predictor is followed by packed four-bit deltas. The source value is a transient Unity instance ID, not a stable identity across runs. New optional fields default to empty or neutral values when reading older schema-1 files. New frames may omit `SceneRenderers` poses that exactly match their geometry snapshot baseline; a later pose restores that baseline when absent. Indexed playback carries earlier stateful animation and visual events with negative window-relative times so their original age remains available at a seek boundary.

`LightSnapshot.EntityId` optionally anchors a light to a recorded entity instead of a scene or ship anchor. Entity and anchor IDs are mutually exclusive. New recordings use this for lights attached to moving items and for a small fallback light on strongly emissive scrap. Older files default to an empty entity ID; playback can derive the fallback light from an item's recorded emissive material. Spider web traps are tracked as transient `web` entities and their mesh is captured in a late world record, so older recordings without a web entity cannot reconstruct when or where a web appeared. For a legacy spider actor missing its body mesh, playback can use the installed game's spider body, materials and rig without saving them into the file.

`GeometrySnapshot.AttachedBonePath` optionally places a rigid mesh such as a spider fang under one skinned actor bone; its geometry pose is local to that bone. An empty path retains the older entity-relative pose. With per-frame bone capture off, a spider frame includes only the game script's procedural mesh-root and leg-joint poses in `EntitySnapshot.Bones`; the remaining bones come from the reusable Animator track. Other enemies use matching installed Animator state and parameters without a bone stream or newly learned tracks.

Static-batched renderers contribute only their own submesh range and compacted referenced vertices. Unowned static-batch slices use world-space vertices with an identity geometry transform, avoiding a second transform or shear approximation. Owned slices remain local to their recorded renderer pose. Distinct batch slices do not share mesh payloads merely because Unity stores them in the same combined buffer.

`IsInterior` and `RoomId` describe generated interior geometry. Playback shows exterior geometry when the spectator camera is outside, and hides it in recorded rooms while showing nearby interiors within a 105 m range. Visibility changes do not execute game occlusion scripts. The capture `WorldVertices` setting defaults to 500,000 unique vertices, accepts at most 1,000,000, and is distinct from the reader's per-geometry limit. Each capture record uses a conservative 45 MiB mesh estimate, leaving headroom in the 96 MiB expanded-record limit for textures and other world fields.

Default appearance limits are 256 textures, 2048 pixels per edge for reading, 32 MiB total PNG bytes per world, 8192 reader materials (1024 recorder materials) and 16 material slots per geometry. The recorder targets up to 2048-pixel ground color layers, 1024-pixel natural/skinned and wall/floor/ceiling primary maps, and lower resolution secondary maps, falling back by halves within a 5 MiB-per-image limit. `TextureSnapshot.FilterMode` stores the source texture's point, bilinear or trilinear filter; absent values in older files keep the previous replay default. The reader checks PNG IHDR dimensions before engine decoding, numeric array lengths and finite values, mesh/bone indices and resource references. Invalid image decoding at playback falls back to material color. Ground textures retain the captured base level at any distance; other textures use mipmaps with preserved alpha-test coverage in foliage. Resources are disposed with their world/viewer.

Frame and event nested timestamps must equal their record timestamp. Times are finite, nonnegative seconds since the current storage part began. Frames are complete snapshots and must have nondecreasing timestamps. Events and worlds can arrive out of timestamp order; the reader sorts them stably, retaining their recorded order at equal times. Entity IDs must be unique within a frame, geometry IDs within a world, and bone paths and renderer IDs within an entity. A missing entity disappears at the first frame that omits it. A newly present entity appears at that frame. Duplicate frame timestamps resolve to the final frame at that timestamp.

The writer creates a new file and refuses to overwrite an existing path. `TryWrite` transfers ownership of the record and its nested objects; callers must not mutate them after acceptance. Its bounded queue returns `false` without dropping an already accepted record when full, disposed, or failed. A recorder should stop and surface the condition. `CompleteAsync` drains accepted records, writes an end marker and closes the stream without waiting on the game frame; `Dispose` waits for that completion. Background serialization/I/O errors are exposed through `Error`, including after disposal, and omit the success end marker. An I/O failure can necessarily prevent already queued records from reaching disk.

The reader recovers the complete prefix when the final length prefix or compressed payload is physically truncated. A missing end marker produces a warning. Invalid complete records, unsupported schemas, invalid transforms, impossible mesh indices, and exceeded limits raise `InvalidDataException`; corruption is never silently treated as valid data. JSON type metadata is ignored, and the parser never instantiates arbitrary types from file contents. Each expanded size is bounded before decompression, and the actual expanded size must match its declaration.

The ordinary full-session reader retains a 1 GiB file/512 MiB aggregate limit, with 48 MiB compressed / 96 MiB expanded per record, 500,000 records, 432,000 frames, 1,000,000 total entity snapshots, 4,096 entities per frame, 512 bones and 512 renderer poses per entity, 100,000 events, 256 worlds, and seven days of elapsed time. `ReplayReadLimits` exposes these limits. New single-file playback uses a separate bounded index (16 GiB physical, 24 GiB expanded, 2,000,000 records and 1,000,000 frames) and loads approximately 48 MiB of frame/event data plus the latest world set per virtual window, under the ordinary 512 MiB in-memory window limit.

`ReplayTimeline.Sample` binary-searches the surrounding frames, linearly interpolates position and scale, and spherically interpolates rotation along the shortest quaternion arc. It preserves the left frame's discrete state until the next timestamp. It does not interpolate across a missing entity, kind change, or activation change. Matching active renderer poses interpolate their local transforms, allowing a stationary door entity's panel to rotate or slide. Renderer visibility and presence change at frame boundaries. The returned snapshot is independent of the loaded data. Queries outside the frame range clamp to the first or final pose.

## Whole-recording time and duration indexing

`ReplayRecordingTimeline` assigns each ordered part a global offset. With valid start timestamps, an offset is the later of its recorded start relative to the first part and the preceding part's end. Missing/earlier timestamps concatenate after the preceding part. This preserves capture gaps without overlapping the playback clock. `Locate` selects the part at a global timestamp; `LocalTime` clamps to that part's available duration, holding its final pose during a gap. Exact boundaries select the new part, and zero-duration parts do not trap seeking. The timeline accepts up to 10,000 distinct paths and seven days total.

`ReplayReader.ReadDuration` first validates the bounded header, then walks record lengths without decoding frame or texture payloads. A valid final end record supplies the completed duration. Incomplete files fall back to the bounded prefix-recovery reader. Invalid lengths and aggregate limits are rejected, and cancellation is observed during reads. This index operation does not substitute for full payload validation when a part is actually loaded.

Legacy playback keeps one active decoded part and at most one prefetched part. New single-file playback uses the same viewer with in-memory windows that share one disk path. Global UI time is converted to local window time for state/event lookup; displayed event timestamps include the window's global offset. The format remains schema 1, and the quota/day file layout is described separately in [ARCHIVE.md](ARCHIVE.md).
